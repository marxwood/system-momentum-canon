# State

This document defines **State** as the canonical concept
for the time-indexed, attributable condition of a system.

State exists between canonical operation and representation:
Mindframe defines the epistemic jurisdictions through which a system may operate,
while Projections derive representations from the condition that operation produces.

The canonical meaning of State is universal.
A concrete state instance is always bound to a particular system and time.

---

## 1. Canonical Status

State is a **canonical foundational construct**.

The Canon defines what State means and which invariants it must preserve.
It does not define the concrete content of any particular system state.

State is:

- time-indexed
- system-bound
- attributable
- reconstructable
- provenance-bearing
- semantically contextual
- non-equivalent to reality or truth

State is not a derivative capability family.
State is the condition over which canonical capabilities operate
and from which non-authoritative representations may be derived.

---

## 2. Why State Exists

State exists to prevent four systemic failures:

1. **Implementation-defined state**  
   A database, agent memory, or runtime representation silently determines
   what the system treats as authoritative condition.

2. **Projection-state collapse**  
   A readable representation is treated as the condition it represents.

3. **Reality-state collapse**  
   The system treats its recorded condition as objective reality.

4. **Memory-state collapse**  
   Historical records and current condition are treated as the same thing.

Without a canonical State concept,
implementations are forced to invent the meaning of system condition,
and presentation or storage may acquire authority by accident.

---

## 3. Definition

**State is the time-indexed, canonically determinable condition of a system,
constituted by the canonical objects, relations, semantic contexts,
validity outcomes, commitments, obligations, authority effects,
provenance, and unresolved conditions attributable to that system at that time.**

State may be incomplete.
State may contain contradiction.
State may contain uncertainty.
State may include claims with different validity outcomes under different regimes.

State does not require forced consensus.

A system may therefore have one attributable State
while preserving multiple claims, interpretations, regime outcomes,
and unresolved contradictions within it.

---

## 4. State and Reality

State is not objective reality.

A system contacts reality through signals, observations, evidence,
reports, and other admissible inputs.
Those inputs may be incomplete, mistaken, manipulated, or contested.

State records what is attributable to the system
under declared semantic, validity, provenance, intent, and authority conditions.
It does not convert system contact into universal truth.

The canonical relation is:

> reality contact → admissible system objects and acts → State → Projections

State is accountable system condition,
not a metaphysical claim about reality itself.

---

## 5. State Constituents

State may include:

- admitted canonical objects
- declared relations among objects
- semantic contexts and KSA versions
- claims and their regime-scoped validity outcomes
- provenance and responsibility records
- commitments and obligations
- binding and governance effects
- active, contested, invalidated, retired, or superseded statuses
- unresolved contradictions and uncertainty
- declared Intent associated with attributable acts
- temporal markers required to determine condition at a given time

This is a canonical conceptual set,
not an implementation schema.

Implementations may represent State differently,
but they must preserve the distinctions and invariants defined here.

---

## 6. State Transitions

A **State transition** is an attributable change
from one system condition to another.

A legitimate State transition must:

- arise from a canonically admissible act
- preserve provenance
- preserve the applicable semantic and regime context
- identify authority where authority is required
- preserve declared Intent where the act is institutional
- preserve prior referability
- be auditable
- never occur silently

Observation may add attributable material without creating commitment.
Reasoning may produce candidates without changing binding condition.
Projection generation does not change State.
Binding and commitment may change State only under applicable Authority rules.

Invalidation, retirement, and supersession change current State
without erasing the prior condition or its trail.

---

## 7. Relationship to Mindframe

Mindframe defines the epistemic domains
through which State may be observed, interpreted, evaluated,
committed, critiqued, or bound.

State is not a Mindframe domain.

It is the cross-domain condition produced and maintained
when domain responsibilities remain separated.

In particular:

- Perception may contribute observations and provenance.
- Cognition may contribute non-binding candidates.
- KSA determines semantic identity and meaning.
- Validity and Regimes determine contextual claim status.
- IEM preserves commitments, provenance, and accountability over time.
- Reflection evaluates State without rewriting it directly.
- Binding / Governance authorizes attributable obligation where permitted.

No domain may silently treat its local output
as the total or authoritative State of the system.

---

## 8. Relationship to Intent

Intent constrains whether an act is admissible
and whether that act may produce a legitimate State transition.

Intent is not State.
Intent does not modify State by declaration alone.

Where Intent is required,
the attributable State transition must preserve the declared Intent
under which the act occurred.

A system may propose candidate Intent,
but no binding State transition may arise from silently inferred Intent.

---

## 9. Relationship to Memory

State is not memory.

Memory preserves the objects, events, commitments,
evaluations, and provenance from which system condition may be determined.

State expresses the attributable condition of the system at a given time.

Canonical memory must make State reconstructable.
State must not require silent mutation or loss of prior referability.

A current State may change.
The trail by which it changed must remain available.

---

## 10. Relationship to Projections

A Projection is derived from State.

Multiple Projections may be generated from the same State.
They may differ in purpose, scope, audience, structure, or emphasis.

A Projection:

- does not become State by being displayed
- does not modify State by being interacted with
- does not create authority
- must preserve sufficient source context to remain attributable
- must remain revocable and regenerable

A request or interaction occurring through a Projection or Surface
may initiate a candidate act.
Only a separately admissible act may produce a State transition.

State must never be reconstructed solely from a Projection
when canonical memory and provenance are available.

---

## 11. Temporal Discipline

State is always relative to time.

A State statement must be interpretable as:

> the attributable condition of system S at time T,
> under the semantic, regime, validity, intent, and authority contexts then applicable.

Later transitions must not retroactively rewrite prior State.

Corrections may alter the current condition,
but the earlier condition and the correction trail remain referable.

---

## 12. Prohibition of Implementation Concerns

At the Canon level:

- database schemas are prohibited
- event-store designs are prohibited
- state machines are prohibited
- synchronization protocols are prohibited
- caching models are prohibited
- agent-memory implementations are prohibited
- serialization formats are prohibited

The Canon defines the meaning and invariants of State,
not its technical representation.

---

## 13. Minimal State Invariants

The following rules are non-negotiable:

1. **State is system-bound and time-indexed.**
2. **State is not objective reality or universal truth.**
3. **State is attributable to canonical objects, acts, and contexts.**
4. **State transitions are explicit, provenance-bearing, and auditable.**
5. **No Projection may create or modify State.**
6. **No implementation representation may acquire authority by storage alone.**
7. **Invalidation and supersession preserve historical referability.**
8. **Contradiction and uncertainty may remain present in State.**
9. **Binding State transitions require applicable Intent and Authority.**
10. **Concrete State representations remain replaceable implementation choices.**

---

## 14. Stability Clause

The canonical meaning of State is stable by default.

Changes to the canonical State model:

- require explicit Canon amendment
- are versioned at Canon level
- are globally breaking by definition

Silent evolution of the State concept is forbidden.

---

## 15. Non-Equivalence Rules

- State ≠ reality
- State ≠ truth
- State ≠ memory
- State ≠ database state
- State ≠ runtime state
- State ≠ projection
- State ≠ interpretation
- State ≠ commitment
- State ≠ authority
- State ≠ consensus

State is the **time-indexed, attributable condition of a system**—nothing else.
